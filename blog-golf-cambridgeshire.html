Golf in Cambridgeshire:
Downland, the Sacred Nine and a City Base

Updated August 2026  ·  7 min read  ·  By Golf East Anglia

← Back to Journal

Cambridgeshire is the part of East Anglia’s golf that visitors reach last, and it is the part that surprises them most. Two courses of genuine national standing sit within half an hour of one of the world’s great university cities — Gog Magog, the only true downland golf in the region, and Royal Worlington & Newmarket, the nine holes Bernard Darwin called sacred. For anyone flying into London or Stansted, Cambridge is the natural front door to the whole of East Anglia. This is our guide to golf in Cambridgeshire and to using Cambridge as a base.

Why Cambridge works as a golfing base

Two hours from London by road, under an hour from Stansted, and on a direct line north into Suffolk and Norfolk. Cambridge gives a group something the coast cannot: a proper city at the end of the day — college courts, punts on the Cam, restaurants that do not close at nine, and hotel stock built for people who arrive from somewhere else. For a mixed group where not everybody plays, that matters more than any tee time.

Gog Magog Golf Club

Downland · Top 100 England · three miles south of Cambridge

Founded in 1901 on the chalk escarpment south of the city and named for the two hills it occupies — among the highest ground in a famously flat county. The Old Course is ranked inside the Top 100 in England: a classic downland layout on crisp, fast turf with natural contours and wide views across the Fens on a clear day. The Wandlebury Course provides a complementary modern test on the same chalk.

What makes it worth the journey is the ground itself. Chalk downland produces firm, running conditions that bear no resemblance to the parkland most visitors expect inland, and it demands a proper ground game. It is the only golf of its kind in East Anglia.

Access: weekday visitor tee times with advance booking; weekends are more restricted, so groups should book well ahead.

Royal Worlington & Newmarket

Heathland · nine holes · Top 100 GB&I

Founded 1893 on forty acres of Breckland heathland, and the course the University of Cambridge team have played since its earliest days — which is why it belongs to the Cambridge golf story even though it sits just over the Suffolk border at Worlington, near Bury St Edmunds. Nine holes, played twice, and widely regarded as the finest nine-hole course anywhere in the world.

It is compact, precise and asks for every club in the bag. Played round a second time the wind has usually turned and the examination is a different one. Generations of golfers have made the drive out from the city to play it, and the drive is still the right way to do it. Our full guide to Royal Worlington goes into more detail.

Newmarket Golf Club

Heathland · Newmarket Heath

A heathland course on Newmarket Heath, the home of British horse racing, and its setting is the whole point — fast-draining sandy soil, wide views, and racehorses working the gallops in the early morning. It is one of the most distinctive golf environments in East Anglia and it sits a few miles from Royal Worlington, which makes the two a natural pairing for a single day.

Cambridge Golf Club

Parkland · Bar Hill, north of the city

A well-maintained parkland course north of Cambridge with open visitor access and good facilities — the most convenient course for a group based in the city centre, and a sensible arrival-day or departure-day round rather than a headline one.

How to build a Cambridgeshire golf trip

Cambridgeshire is not a week on its own, and we would not sell it as one. It is two exceptional courses and a good city, which makes it either a strong two-day opening to a longer East Anglia trip or a self-contained long weekend. The shape that works:

  • Day one — arrive, Cambridge Golf Club or an afternoon in the city, dinner in town
  • Day two — Gog Magog, both courses if the group wants thirty-six
  • Day three — east to Royal Worlington, with Newmarket alongside it

From there the county opens outward. Suffolk’s heathland is directly east — Aldeburgh and Thorpeness on the Heritage Coast, covered in our Suffolk guide. Norfolk’s clifftop links are north. A group that starts in Cambridge and finishes at Brancaster has played downland, heathland and links inside one week, which is a genuinely unusual thing to be able to say in England.

Where to stay in Cambridge

The city carries the accommodation, and it is better stocked than anywhere else in the three counties. University Arms on Parker’s Piece is the landmark address. Hotel du Vin and The Varsity sit centrally, and Hotel Felix is a short way out with more space around it — often the easier option for a group with cars and clubs.

When to come

Chalk downland and Breckland heathland both drain quickly, so Cambridgeshire is playable in all seasons and holds up in weather that closes heavier courses. May to June and September to October are the best of it. Note that Cambridge itself is busiest in high summer and around graduation, which affects hotel rates rather than the golf — a September trip is usually better value on both counts.

The honest summary

Gog Magog and Royal Worlington are the reasons to come, and they are reasons enough. The supporting courses are pleasant rather than remarkable, and we would rather say that than pad a three-day itinerary to look impressive. Cambridgeshire’s real strength is as the gateway: it is the easiest arrival point in East Anglia, it offers golf you cannot get elsewhere in the region, and everything else is upstream of it.
