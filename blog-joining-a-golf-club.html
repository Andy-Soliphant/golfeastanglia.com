What Joining a Golf Club
Really Means

September 2026  ·  4 min read  ·  By Andy Carroll

← Back to Journal

Golf has been life-changing for us. It has become my wife’s pastime and my son’s too, and we play most weeks together, often more than once. It is time away from the modern world of screens, spent enjoying the world as it was — and perhaps as it should be. I won’t get philosophical about it. Not yet, at least.

Like a lot of social golfers, I never found the idea of joining a club particularly appealing. The narrative rings out: I like to play different courses. And with good reason — variety is the spice of life. The cost is real too. People balk at the annual fees, and in the financial world we are living in that is entirely understandable.

But what I have found at Royal Cromer is so much more than rounds of golf. It is an extended family.

Yes, I get to play whenever I like. That is the tip of the iceberg, and it is the part I never grasped until now. You can play on your own whenever you want — but you are also welcomed into the club, and into club life. With a little effort you find yourself invited to all kinds of events, games and functions.

And why? Because you are among people who think the way you do. They love the game. It is why they keep coming back in all weather, just as you do. You share the same stories and the same memories, and they bind you together.

You have all suffered at the hands of this game — and you have all had the moments of pure joy that only the people who truly get it can appreciate.

The social side runs right through the club. My wife has been invited into the ladies’ events — not only golf, but afternoon tea, wine evenings, and no shortage of offers to play. My son has loved being part of a club team, and reaching a final this year you could see exactly how much it meant to him. Walking down a fairway with a couple of the older boys, chatting away, swinging imaginary clubs as he went — it was one of the best feelings. The golf was almost secondary.

Almost.

The club organises so much, for so many, young and old. Interclub matches. Away days. Sunday roast. A music quiz and other winter events to keep everyone entertained when the dark nights roll in. And a team behind the bar who greet you warmly however your round went, like friends, taking the edge off what might have been a poor day.

I soon found myself making friends I didn’t know I needed, and watching my wife and son do the same. The club becomes a second home — a place you want to be, surrounded by people you want to be around.

Joining a golf club isn’t really about playing golf. That is almost a byproduct. Joining the right golf club is like finding lost relatives and sitting down to a Sunday roast with a fine red in front of the fire.

It is a sense of coming home.
