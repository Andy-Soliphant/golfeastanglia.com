By Arrangement

Seven nights.
Two coasts.
One road in.

A week on the east coast of England, arranged entirely around you. Tee sheets closed for the morning, a helicopter north from London, and a car and driver for the duration.

Most of what is sold as exclusive golf is simply expensive golf. A better hotel, a longer itinerary, the same tee times as everyone else.

By Arrangement is how we work when none of that is the point. Nothing is packaged and nothing is priced in advance, because each of these is built from the ground up around a particular group, a particular week, and what that group actually wants from it.

On this coast that means we can close a course for a morning, put you on the first tee before anyone else has arrived, and have you sitting down to dinner in a clubhouse that does not ordinarily serve it. It means a week built outward from the tide at Brancaster rather than squeezed around it. And it means saying so plainly when something is not possible on your dates, which is the part most people leave out.

Norfolk and Suffolk are the last stretch of great English links golf that has not been organised for visitors. That is precisely why it can still be arranged this way — and why, for now, almost nobody has.

How it works

You tell us the shape. Dates, numbers, how hard you want to play, and who is travelling with you who would rather not.

We find out what is actually possible. Which mornings can be held, what the tide allows that week, whether the aircraft and the houses line up. That takes days rather than minutes, and it is the part you are paying for.

Then we put it to you whole. One arrangement, nothing itemised, and nothing left for you to organise on arrival.

The East Coast Week

London, the north Norfolk coast and Suffolk. Royal Cromer closed for a morning, Brancaster timed to the tide, and Royal Worlington to finish. The one we have set out in full.

The Long Season

Nine nights

The same coast with two more nights in it, which changes it more than it sounds. A day with no tee time on it, the Brecks given a movement of their own, and two mornings on the Suffolk coast rather than one squeezed against a departure. The version we would choose for a group where not everyone plays.

The Ryder Cup week

September 2027

The days either side. The 2027 match is at Adare Manor, and most Americans crossing for it have two or three empty days at one end or the other — usually spent waiting. We take those days instead: Cromer closed for a morning, Brancaster timed to the tide, and a car to the airport when it is time to go. The Sacred Nine

Four nights

Built around Royal Worlington, nine holes inland that a great many good judges call the finest nine in the world. For the golfer who has played the famous courses and would rather see the ones that are merely perfect.

Three nights, uncompromised

The north coast

The same standard over a long weekend rather than a week — Cromer, Sheringham and Brancaster on the tide, with everything else unchanged. For those who cannot give the east coast seven days but will not give it less than its due.

The one thing money cannot move

Brancaster runs on the tide.

The road to Royal West Norfolk crosses a tidal marsh. At high water it is under the sea, and the club is unreachable — for members, for visitors, for everyone. Golf here is scheduled around the water twice a day, every day, and has been since 1892.

Every trip we arrange on this coast is built outward from that window. It is also, we think, the most honest thing about Norfolk golf: there is one door, it opens twice a day, and nobody holds the key.

What we arrange

Specifics, not adjectives.

A course to yourselves

Tee sheets closed for a morning, with a starter on the first. Arranged at the club's discretion and ours, and never sold as a certainty before it is held.

Arrival by air, as standard

Not an upgrade to be asked for. You fly north from London to the clifftop helipad at Cromer, a short walk from the golf. If the weather says otherwise — and on this coast it sometimes does — your car is already waiting and nothing else about the day changes.

A car and driver, throughout

Not on call. Simply there, for the whole week — between the two coasts, to dinner, and to Holkham with anyone who would rather not play. Clubs move separately, so nobody handles a bag between the first tee and the last green.

Dining inside the club

Fine dining in clubhouses that do not ordinarily offer it, and tables held at the three Michelin-starred restaurants on this coast.

Caddies and coaching

Caddies arranged in advance. A PGA professional with you for the trip, if a group would rather play better by Sunday than merely play.

The rest of it

Shooting, sailing on the marshes, a seal trip from Blakeney, a day at Holkham. For those travelling with people who do not play — the ten places we send them.

Where you stay

Five houses, two coasts.

Langham

The Harper

Thirty-two rooms in the converted flint barns of an old glassworks. A serious spa, a Michelin Key, and residents only throughout — which is why it stays quiet even when full.

Morston

Morston Hall

A seventeenth-century flint house on the marsh edge that has held a Michelin star for twenty-five years. Six rooms. The dinner you build an evening around.

Thorpe Market

The Gunton Arms

Rooms in a shooting lodge set in a deer park, steaks over an open fire, and a collection of art on the walls that would be at home in a gallery. Helicopter arrivals accepted.

Suffolk

Wilderness Reserve

Exclusive-use country houses across a five-thousand-acre estate, taken whole rather than by the room. The natural base for a group who would rather have the place to themselves.

Aldeburgh

The Brudenell

On the shingle at Aldeburgh, a few minutes from both golf courses, with the sea directly outside the window. Simpler than the others, and the right answer for some weeks.
