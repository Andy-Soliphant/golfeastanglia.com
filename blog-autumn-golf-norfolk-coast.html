Autumn Golf on the Norfolk Coast:
The Season the Locals Keep Quiet

July 2026  ·  6 min read  ·  By Golf East Anglia

← Back to Journal

Ask anyone who plays their golf on the Norfolk coast when the courses are at their best, and you'll get the same slightly reluctant answer — as if they'd rather you didn't know. It isn't high summer. It's the long, golden run from September into November, when the crowds thin out, the light turns, and the coast quietly hands you its finest golf of the year.

More golf, fewer people, and better value — that is the autumn trade on this coast.

Why autumn plays best

After a dry end to summer the fairways firm up and start to run, and links and heathland golf is meant to be played on ground like this — the ball chasing, the bounces honest, the approach asking you to think rather than simply launch. The greens have settled after a season of play, and there's usually enough wind off the North Sea to keep you honest without the raw bite of winter.

The light does half the work. Low autumn sun across a clifftop fairway, the sea turning silver behind the greens, the heather still holding its colour inland — it's the kind of morning that makes you glad you booked the early tee.

And you get the place to yourself. The summer visitors have gone home, tee times open up, the clubhouse is warm, and green fees are kinder than they are in July. The North Norfolk towns turn cosy, too — fresh crab, proper pubs with a fire going, and big empty beaches for the walk between rounds.

The courses at their best

Play two or three across a long weekend and you'll have had the full range of what this coast does — clifftop, heathland and links — in the season that shows each of them off.

Royal Cromer

Clifftop golf as it's meant to be, perched high above the North Sea with the lighthouse for company. Founded in 1888 and Royal by royal patronage, it offers one of the finest views in English golf — and never better than under an autumn sky.

Sheringham

Plays right along the edge of the coast, the little North Norfolk steam railway puffing below the elevated holes and the sea filling the horizon. Exposed, honest, and unforgettable.

Thetford

A complete change of character just inland: classic Breckland heathland of ancient pines and heather, firm and tree-lined, and beautiful in October colour.

Royal West Norfolk (Brancaster)

Timeless championship links out on the tidal marshes — wild, old-school, and one of the great experiences in the game.
