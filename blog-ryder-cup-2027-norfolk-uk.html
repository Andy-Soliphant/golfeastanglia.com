Ryder Cup 2027:
Golf in the Morning, the Cup in the Afternoon

August 2026  ·  6 min read  ·  By Golf East Anglia

← Back to Journal

The centenary Ryder Cup is at Adare Manor from 17 to 19 September 2027. A hundred years after the first match, back in Ireland for the first time since 2006, and by some distance the most wanted three days in European golf. Which is the problem. The ballot has already been through one window, general admission is priced accordingly, and even with a ticket you are looking at flights, a week's accommodation in County Limerick and a fortnight's planning to watch golf you could watch better on television.

There is a version of that weekend that costs a fraction of it and gives you more golf. You play the Norfolk coast in the morning and you watch the Ryder Cup in the afternoon, with a pint in your hand and nobody standing in front of you. Groups are already booking it with us for September 2027, and it is worth explaining why the maths works.

The shape of the weekend

Match play at Adare Manor runs Friday to Sunday. European afternoons, so nothing meaningful happens before lunch on any of the three days. That leaves every morning free, and a morning is exactly enough for eighteen holes on a links course if you are staying within twenty minutes of it.

Three mornings, three courses, three afternoons in front of the golf. You are not choosing between playing and watching. That is the whole idea, and it only works because of where Norfolk's courses sit relative to each other.

Which three courses

The obvious run is the north coast. Royal Cromer on the clifftop, Sheringham along the same stretch, and Hunstanton or Royal West Norfolk to the west if the group wants the serious links test. All three are within a short drive of each other and of somewhere decent to stay, which is the part that makes early tee times bearable.

Mid-September is also when Norfolk is at its best. The summer visitors have gone, the courses have had a full season of growth and are running fast, and the light on that coast in September is the reason people photograph it. Green fees are outside peak. It is a better time to play here than August, and it costs less.

Where you watch it

This matters more than people expect. A Ryder Cup afternoon is three or four hours and it wants a room with a proper screen, decent food arriving without a fuss, and no one changing the channel. Some clubhouses will do this well and some will not, and it is worth arranging in advance rather than turning up hopefully.

We book it as part of the trip — the room, the table, the food timed around the golf rather than against it. Several of the places we use on that coast are happy to put a group in front of the television for the afternoon if they know you are coming.

Why book it now rather than next spring

September 2027 is thirteen months away, which sounds like plenty. It is not, for two reasons.

The first is accommodation. The north Norfolk coast has a small number of good places and they are not large. A group of eight or twelve needs rooms in one building, and that is the constraint that bites first — well before tee times do.

The second is that Ryder Cup weekend is not a normal September weekend. Every golf club in the country will have members thinking the same thought, and the clubs that host visitors well will fill. Groups booking with us now are doing it because they have been through this before with a major and found out the hard way.

What it costs against going to Ireland

We will not pretend a Norfolk weekend is the same experience as standing on the first tee at Adare Manor. It is not. But the comparison people actually face is not Norfolk against Adare Manor — it is Norfolk against sitting at home, because tickets are scarce and expensive and most people will not get them.

Against that, three nights on the Norfolk coast with three rounds of golf and every afternoon of the Ryder Cup is not a consolation. For a lot of groups it is the better weekend, and it is one you can actually book.

How we arrange it

We are in Norfolk, we play these courses, and we book the tee times, the rooms and the transport as one trip. You tell us the size of the group and roughly what standard of golf you want, and we come back with a shape. There is no charge for that conversation.

For groups wanting something further than a standard weekend — a course to yourselves in the morning, or a longer week taking in both coasts — that is arranged rather than booked, and we handle it under By Arrangement. The ordinary weekend is still the ordinary weekend, and we are just as glad to sort that out.

Ryder Cup 2027 in Norfolk: common questions

When exactly is the 2027 Ryder Cup?
The matches are Friday 17 to Sunday 19 September 2027 at Adare Manor, County Limerick. The full event week including practice days runs from Monday 13 September. It is the centenary edition, a hundred years after the first match in 1927, and the 46th time it has been contested.

Can we play golf and still watch every session?
Yes. The competition sessions run through European afternoons, so mornings are clear on all three days. Eighteen holes with an early tee time leaves you back in front of a screen before anything is decided.

How far in advance should we book?
Now, if the group is more than four. Accommodation on the north Norfolk coast is the limiting factor rather than tee times, and the good places take group bookings well over a year ahead.

Which Norfolk courses would we play?
Typically Royal Cromer, Sheringham and one of Hunstanton or Royal West Norfolk, depending on the group's handicaps and what you want out of it. All are within a comfortable drive of the same base.

Do you arrange somewhere to watch the golf?
Yes, as part of the booking — a room with a proper screen and food timed around the afternoon sessions rather than against them.
