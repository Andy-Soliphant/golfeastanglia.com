Winter Golf in England:
The Norfolk Coast from London

September 2026  ·  5 min read  ·  By Golf East Anglia

← Back to Journal

If you play golf in the northern United States, your season probably ends in the fall. In England it doesn’t. The climate is milder, and the seaside courses in the east of the country are built on sand and play all winter, much as they have for more than a century.

That is worth knowing if you are already coming to England, whether for business in London, a family visit over the holidays or a stopover on the way elsewhere. Norfolk is the closest stretch of genuine links golf to London, and two or three days on the coast fit easily into a trip you are already making.

Getting there from London

Trains run from London Liverpool Street to Norwich in under two hours. A local line continues to Cromer and Sheringham on the coast in about three quarters of an hour. By car it is roughly three hours from central London, and we can arrange a driver.

Traditional English winter golf

Royal Cromer, Sheringham, Hunstanton and Royal West Norfolk at Brancaster are true links, on sandy ground with dunes or clifftops and hardly a tree. The sand drains naturally, so the courses play firm and fast through the winter. The ball runs, the wind is part of the game, and it is the kind of golf many Americans travel to Scotland or Ireland to find.

Your World Handicap System index from home is accepted, and most clubs will ask to see it.

The light and the welcome

The winter light on this coast is low, bright and beautiful. You tee off mid-morning and finish in the afternoon sun off the sea.

Winter is the quiet season, and a visitor from America is a welcome surprise.

The welcome is what visitors talk about afterwards. The clubs have time for guests, the pubs have a fire going, and you are a welcome surprise rather than one more face in the queue. Tee times are easier to find, and rates at most clubs and hotels are lower than in summer.

Quieter towns to explore

The north Norfolk towns are at their most peaceful in winter: Georgian Holt, Cromer’s Victorian pier, the harbor towns of Blakeney and Wells, and Holkham’s wide, empty beach. Grey seals pup at Blakeney Point over the winter months, and it is one of the best places in England to see them.

What we’d suggest

For two or three days, base yourself on the north coast around Cromer. Play Royal Cromer and Sheringham, ten minutes apart, and add Brancaster or Hunstanton for a third day. Stay somewhere with a fire and a good dining room, and you will be back in London in time for whatever brought you over.
