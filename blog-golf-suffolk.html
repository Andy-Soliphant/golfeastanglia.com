Golf in Suffolk:
Heathland, Links and the Sacred Nine

Updated August 2026  ·  8 min read  ·  By Golf East Anglia

← Back to Journal

Suffolk plays a different game from Norfolk. Where the north Norfolk coast gives you exposed clifftop links and salt marsh, Suffolk gives you heathland — gorse, heather, silver birch and fast-draining sandy soil, on courses laid out by the men who shaped British golf between the wars. It is quieter, less written about, and for a certain kind of golfer it is the better week. This is our guide to the golf courses in Suffolk, and to how a trip here is best put together.

The Suffolk golf courses, at a glance

Suffolk’s golf divides neatly into three clusters, and understanding that is most of the planning:

  • The Heritage Coast — Aldeburgh and Thorpeness, two miles apart, plus Felixstowe Ferry further south
  • West Suffolk — Royal Worlington & Newmarket and Flempton, both within a short drive of Bury St Edmunds
  • Central and south — Woodbridge, Ipswich (Purdis Heath) and Fynn Valley, useful as connectors between the two

Aldeburgh and Thorpeness: the two-course pairing that makes the trip

Heathland · Suffolk coast · two miles apart

If you only have one day in Suffolk, this is where you spend it. Aldeburgh Golf Club sits inside the Top 100 of Britain and Ireland, with a design pedigree that reads like a roll of honour — Willie Park Jr, JH Taylor and Harry Colt. It is maritime heathland that plays with a genuine links feel: gorse-lined, fast-draining, and a par 68 that is a far sterner examination than the card suggests. Foursomes are the preferred game and the club is unapologetic about it.

Thorpeness Golf Club is barely two miles up the road — a James Braid layout from 1922 that has been left largely as he drew it, running through silver birch, pine, gorse and heather, with the House in the Clouds standing above the tree line. It has a hotel on site, which makes it the natural base for the pair.

Two courses of this quality within two miles of each other is unusual anywhere in England. It means a group can play thirty-six holes in a day without a transfer worth the name, and it is the single strongest argument for a Suffolk trip.

Royal Worlington & Newmarket — the Sacred Nine

Heathland · west Suffolk · nine holes played twice

Bernard Darwin called it the sacred nine in 1928 and the name stuck. Forty acres of west Suffolk heathland, founded in 1893, consistently ranked in the Top 100 of Britain and Ireland — and widely held to be the finest nine-hole course in the world. Cambridge University’s golfers have played here since the club’s earliest days.

Most visitors play it twice round, and the second nine is not the first nine repeated: the wind shifts, the pins move, and the examination changes. It is a course serious golfers travel specifically to see, and it is the natural closing round of an East Anglia trip rather than an opening one.

Pair it with Flempton, a nine-hole JH Taylor design from 1906 a few miles away, and west Suffolk gives you a full day of golf that almost nobody outside the county has played.

Felixstowe Ferry — the only true links in Suffolk

Links · Felixstowe · founded 1880

The Martello Course at Felixstowe Ferry is one of England’s oldest, founded in 1880 and named for the tower standing between the fourteenth tee and the penultimate green. It is fully exposed to the North Sea and it is the only genuine links golf in the county. When the wind is up here, it is up.

It sits a little apart from the other clusters, so it works best as an arrival or departure round for groups coming through Ipswich or Felixstowe rather than as a mid-week addition.

The inland courses worth your time

Woodbridge Golf Club, founded 1893, is one of the county’s most respected — heathland through pine and heather with tight, well-defined fairways, and it sits conveniently between the coast and Ipswich. Ipswich Golf Club at Purdis Heath is a James Braid design from 1926 that uses undulating heathland properly, and is well regarded for its condition. Fynn Valley near Ipswich has a driving range, a nine-hole course and self-catering cottages, which makes it useful for groups who want practice facilities on site.

How to build a Suffolk golf trip

The geography does the work if you let it. A three-night trip we would recommend without hesitation:

  • Day one — arrive and play Woodbridge or Ipswich on the way through, then on to the coast
  • Day two — Aldeburgh in the morning, Thorpeness in the afternoon, or split them across two mornings if you would rather have your afternoons back
  • Day three — west to Royal Worlington, with Flempton alongside it if the group has the legs

That is three genuinely distinct kinds of golf inside a county you can cross in an hour. And because Suffolk sits directly below Norfolk, it extends naturally: the clifftop links at Royal Cromer, Sheringham, Hunstanton and Brancaster are a straightforward onward leg, which is how most of our longer East Anglia weeks are built.

Where to stay

Thorpeness has rooms on site, which solves the coastal pairing in one move. The Brudenell at Aldeburgh puts you on the seafront within walking distance of the town. For a group wanting the whole place to themselves, Wilderness Reserve offers exclusive-use houses in the Suffolk countryside — a different proposition entirely, and one we arrange through By Arrangement.

When to come

Heathland drains well, which is Suffolk’s quiet advantage — the courses stay playable when heavier ground elsewhere does not. May and June give you the best of the gorse and the longest light. September and early October give you firmer turf, quieter tee sheets and the easiest availability of the year for a group. Winter golf is genuinely viable here in a way it is not everywhere.

An honest word about Suffolk

Suffolk is not a links county. If your group has flown in wanting to play the kind of golf that made Britain famous, the north Norfolk coast is where that lives, and we will tell you so. What Suffolk offers instead is heathland of real quality, laid out by Braid, Colt, Taylor and Park, with a fraction of the traffic and none of the fuss. Several of our clients who came for Norfolk now come back for Suffolk.
