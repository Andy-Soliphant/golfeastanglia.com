The long weekend

Two movements.

I

Arrival

The first afternoon

North, and straight to it

Forty-five minutes from London to the clifftop at Cromer, which puts you a short walk from the first tee with the afternoon still ahead of you. On a three-night trip that hour matters more than it does on a seven-night one — it is the difference between three playing days and two.

If the coast has other ideas, the car is already waiting and the day runs as written, a little later.

Nine holes, or none

Royal Cromer in the late light if the group wants it, or a walk on the cliff and dinner if it does not. Neither is a compromise. The first evening here is worth arriving into rather than sleeping through.

II

Three mornings

Built around one tide
One

Royal West Norfolk, on the tide

Brancaster, and the only fixed point in the whole weekend. The road across the marsh is under water twice a day and there is no second entrance, so this morning is not ours to schedule — it is set by the tide table, months out, and the other two days move around it. Which is why we ask for your dates before we say anything else.

Two

Royal Cromer, held for the morning

A clifftop course with the lighthouse standing in the middle of it and the sea on your shoulder for most of the round. We ask the club to close the sheet for the morning and put a starter on the first. That is arranged at their discretion and ours, and we do not sell it as certain until it is held.

Three

Sheringham, or Hunstanton

Sheringham is ten minutes west — a clifftop links where the railway runs below the fourth and the sea is in view from nearly every hole. Hunstanton is thirty minutes the other way, and the course a good many people who have played both quietly prefer: dunes, hard ground, and a wind that decides the round.

Which one depends on where the tide has put Brancaster. We will tell you when you tell us the dates.

The afternoon with nothing in it

One thing, done properly.

A long weekend cannot carry a programme, and it should not try. One afternoon is left open, and one thing is arranged into it if the group wants it. Not three.

The seals at Blakeney

The largest grey seal colony in England, out on the point, reached by boat from Morston Quay. Privately, rather than on the shared trip — it is the difference between a pleasant hour and one people talk about. Between November and January they are pupping, which is also the quietest quarter on this coast.

Crab, from the boat that landed it

The crab boats at Cromer still work off the beach. A morning with one of them, then the crab for lunch, is the sort of thing nobody expects and everybody remembers.

Holkham, and the marshes

The hall, the walled garden, and the beach at the end of Lady Anne’s Drive. The obvious answer for anyone in the group who has not come for the golf, and the one they will thank you for.

Or nothing at all

Three days of links golf in a Norfolk wind is enough for most people, and an afternoon in a chair facing the sea is a legitimate use of it. We would not argue.

What is arranged around it

The same as every commission.

Nothing on this page is an upgrade to be asked for. It is what a commission under By Arrangement includes, and the itinerary above is simply where it happens to go.

A car and driver, throughout

Not on call. There for the whole stay — to the courses, to dinner, and to Holkham with anyone who would rather not play. Clubs move separately, so nobody handles a bag between the first tee and the last green.

Dinner worth changing for

Morston Hall has held a Michelin star for twenty-five years and is fifteen minutes from where you are staying. Meadowsweet at Holt and The Neptune at Old Hunstanton are the other two on this coast. Tables are held before you arrive, because in season they are not there to be found on the night.

Caddies and a professional

Caddies arranged in advance where the club has them. A PGA professional with you throughout, for a group who would rather play better by the end than merely play.

For those not playing

Shooting, sailing on the marshes, the seals at Blakeney, a day at Holkham. Arranged to the same standard as the golf, and never as an afterthought — most groups here include somebody who has come for the coast rather than the courses.

How it begins

Tell us your dates and we will tell you the tide.

Three nights is the shortest commission we take, and the tide makes it the least flexible — there are dates in any month when Brancaster simply will not give a workable morning, and we would rather say so at the start than build around it.

Send us the dates and the number, and we will come back with what is actually possible on them, including what is not.
