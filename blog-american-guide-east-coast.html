An American Golfer's Guide
to England's East Coast

June 2026  ·  12 min read  ·  By Golf East Anglia

← Back to Journal

If you golf, you know St Andrews. You probably know Royal Dornoch, Bandon Dunes, maybe Royal County Down. What you almost certainly don't know is that two hours from London sits a stretch of English coastline with four genuine links courses — one of them ranked in the World Top 100 — that has never been properly marketed to an American audience. If you're planning a golf vacation to England, this guide exists to change that.

We're not going to pretend Norfolk and Suffolk are Scotland. They're something else entirely: a quieter, more accessible, more affordable links experience, an hour and a half from a major international airport, with the kind of unhurried English county life around it that most golf trips don't have time for. Here is everything you need to actually plan a trip.

Where is this, exactly?

Norfolk and Suffolk sit on England's east coast, in the historic region known as East Anglia — directly east of Cambridge, about two hours by car or rail from central London. If you've flown into Heathrow or landed at Stansted for a UK golf trip, this region is far closer than Scotland and considerably closer than you'd expect for genuine links golf.

Geographically, think of it as the other side of England from the courses you've heard of. Norfolk faces the North Sea on its north and east coasts; Suffolk continues that coastline south. The result is a string of true links and heathland courses that British golfers have known about for over a century, and that American golfers are only now starting to discover.

The courses you should know

Royal West Norfolk (Brancaster)

A genuine pilgrimage course. Played in foursomes format on a narrow strip of land accessible by a causeway that floods at high tide — yes, you read that correctly, the round is planned around the tide table. Ranked among the World Top 100. Nothing in American golf prepares you for this.

Hunstanton Golf Club

A Top 100 GB&I championship links and the only west-facing course on England's entire east coast, meaning sunset rounds with the light coming in low across The Wash. Firm, fast, and a serious test for any single-figure player.

Royal Cromer

Set on the highest clifftop on England's east coast, with the North Sea visible from almost every hole. One of only 66 clubs in the world to hold Royal patronage.

Aldeburgh and Thorpeness (Suffolk)

Two James Braid heathland classics two miles apart on the Suffolk Heritage Coast. A completely different character of golf — gorse, heather, fast-draining sandy soil — and largely unknown outside the UK.

Royal Worlington

Bernard Darwin, golf writer and grandson of Charles Darwin, called it "the Sacred Nine." Nine holes on forty acres of Breckland heathland, considered by many serious golfers to be the finest nine-hole course anywhere in the world.

How this compares to the trips you already know

If you've done Bandon Dunes, you know the appeal of a self-contained golf destination with several world-class courses close together. Norfolk and Suffolk work on a similar principle — Royal West Norfolk, Hunstanton and Royal Cromer are all within thirty miles of each other on the north Norfolk coast — but with the difference that you are also two hours from London, not a connecting flight from anywhere.

If you've done St Andrews or the Scottish links circuit, the comparison is more direct: proper, firm, sea-affected golf, on courses with serious pedigree, without the premium pricing or the crowds that now come with Scotland's most famous names. Green fees here run meaningfully lower than the headline Scottish courses, and visitor tee time availability is, for now, considerably easier to secure.

What you won't get is the brand recognition. Nobody back home will immediately know what Brancaster is. We'd argue that's part of the appeal — this is still genuinely undiscovered ground, in a sport where almost nowhere is left undiscovered.

Planning logistics

Getting there

Fly into Heathrow, Gatwick or Stansted. Stansted is the closest major airport to Suffolk, around 60 miles south of the courses. From Heathrow or central London, Norwich is roughly two and a half hours by car. Norwich also has its own airport with limited European connections if you're routing through the continent.

Currency and cost

We quote in GBP, USD, EUR or AUD on request, so you'll always know exactly what you're spending in dollars before you commit. As a rough guide, a fully guided golf vacation package including golf, accommodation and transfers runs from around $950 USD per person for a three-night introduction up to $3,800 USD per person for the full ten-night Grand Tour of both counties — figures that compare favourably with equivalent Scottish golf trip itineraries once flights and premium green fees are factored in.

Best time to visit

May through September gives you the most reliable weather and the longest daylight hours. The shoulder months of April and October are quieter and often excellent value. The courses are genuinely playable year-round if you don't mind packing for wind and rain — this is, after all, proper links golf.

Language and driving

None of the usual travel friction. English is the first language, road signage is straightforward, and a standard US driving license combined with an International Driving Permit is sufficient to rent a car, though we typically arrange private transfers so you can focus entirely on the golf.

Why book this as a package rather than independently

Beyond that, visitor access policies vary significantly between these clubs, and several operate restricted visitor windows that aren't obvious from a club website built primarily for UK members.
